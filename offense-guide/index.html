    Traditional Motion vs. Read & React

    1. After you pass the ball

    • Traditional Motion: You make a choice. After you pass, you can choose to go set a screen for a teammate on the other side of the floor.
    • Read & React: You don't choose. After you pass, you must sprint directly to the basket. You have to clear out so someone else can fill your spot.

    2. When screens happen

    • Traditional Motion: You have to read your defender. If they trail behind you, you curl toward the basket. If they cheat under the screen, you flare out for a shot.
    • Read & React: The rules make the choice for you. You don't have to guess what the defender is doing. The system tells you exactly where to go based on the type of screen being set.

    3. When someone dribbles right at you

    • Traditional Motion: You and your teammate have to be on the same page. It could mean you cut backdoor, it could mean you swap spots, or it could mean you take a handoff.
    • Read & React: It always means one thing. If a teammate dribbles toward you, you cut to the basket immediately. No guessing, no waiting.

    4. When the ball goes into the post

    • Traditional Motion: The players on the outside swap spots or screen for each other to keep their defenders busy while the post player works.
    • Read & React: The person who passed the ball sprints right past the post player looking for a quick handoff or a layup. Everyone else waits for that first cut to happen.

            Grinnell Target Metrics

            Per-game benchmarks that define a successful Grinnell performance. If the numbers are right, the system is working — regardless of whether every possession looks clean.

            Shot Attempts94+ per game

            Volume is the foundation. Force the game into a numbers battle where depth and conditioning compound.

            3-Point Attempts47–50+ per game

            Only threes and layups. No midrange. The math favors the team that eliminates the 2-point pull-up.

            Offensive Rebound Rate33%+ per game

            Four players crash hard every shot. Extra possessions extend the volume advantage and keep defenses scrambling.

            Forced Turnovers32+ per game

            Press after every make and miss. More turnovers = more possessions = more shots.

            Shot Attempt Differential+25 to +30

            Create significantly more attempts than the opponent. Shot differential is the real measure of whether the system is generating an advantage.

            Midrange AttemptsNear Zero

            The midrange shot is actively discouraged. Any midrange attempt is a failed possession — hunt threes or attack the rim.

            Shot Distribution Target

            3-Pointers — 60% of all shots
            Layups & at-rim — 38% of all shots
            Midrange — 2% (near zero by design)
