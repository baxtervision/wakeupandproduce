Basketball vocabulary

Key Terms

A focused varsity vocabulary for teaching systems, calling coverages, and building shared language across your roster. Each term is written so players can hear it in practice and apply it on the next possession.

Varsity Cheat Sheet

If you only remember ten defensive terms, remember these. They show up in every system, every scout, and every timeout huddle.

Defensive Foundations

Defense

On-ball defense

Guarding the player with the ball while staying in stance, matching the scout, and making every catch, dribble, and shot uncomfortable.

Coach point: Distance changes by personnel: crowd shooters, cushion drivers.

Player cue: Ball pressure without a blow-by.

Contain

Keeping the ball in front and giving ground if needed so the offense does not get a straight-line drive.

Coach point: The first job is to stop the lane, not win the steal.

Player cue: Chest between ball and rim.

Closeout

Sprint-to-short-chop recovery to a shooter, arriving balanced with high hands and no fly-by.

Coach point: Close short to drivers and long to shooters.

Player cue: High hands, low hips.

Stance

Low, balanced defensive posture with knees bent, hips loaded, chest up, and active hands.

Coach point: Stance is the floor for every coverage.

Player cue: Sit down before the ball moves.

Slide / Drop step

Lateral footwork to stay square, with a drop step used to recover, turn, and cut off the ball when beaten.

Coach point: Slide while square; drop step when the angle is lost.

Player cue: No crossed feet until recovery.

Help defense

Leaving your assignment while seeing ball and player to stop a drive, protect the paint, or buy time for a teammate.

Coach point: Help early enough that the drive sees a body.

Player cue: See both, shrink first.

Gap

The space between the ball and your player where you sit one or two steps off to discourage penetration.

Coach point: One pass away can help without fully abandoning the pass.

Player cue: One foot in the lane line of the drive.

Deny

Taking away the next pass by putting your body and hand in the passing lane.

Coach point: Deny when the catch hurts you; gap when the drive hurts you.

Player cue: Hand on the line, body on the player.

Shell principles

The basic team rules of guard ball, jump to the ball, help the helper, and move on airtime.

Coach point: Shell is not a drill only; it is the team defense operating system.

Player cue: Move while the pass is flying.

Pack line / Pressure

Pack line keeps off-ball defenders inside an imaginary arc to protect the paint; pressure extends defenders into the offense to disrupt rhythm.

Coach point: Both can work if all five players understand the same risk.

Player cue: Know whether we are shrinking or speeding them up.

Screen & Pick-and-Roll Coverage

Coverages

Ball screen

An on-ball screen set for the ballhandler, including high, side, flat, drag, and empty-side pick-and-rolls.

Coach point: Coverage call must come before contact.

Player cue: Talk early, hit first.

Hedge

The screener's defender jumps out toward the ball to slow or turn the handler, then recovers back to the screener.

Coach point: Soft hedge contains; hard hedge turns the ball.

Player cue: Show your chest, then sprint back.

Blitz / Trap

Both defenders attack the ballhandler off a screen to force a pickup, rushed pass, or turnover.

Coach point: Best near sidelines, late clock, or against shaky handlers.

Player cue: No split, no middle, hands high.

Switch

Two defenders exchange assignments on a screen so the ball stays covered without chasing through contact.

Coach point: Switch only works with loud talk and instant body contact.

Player cue: Call it, touch it, own it.

Drop coverage

The screener's defender stays back between roller and rim while the on-ball defender fights over or around the screen.

Coach point: Protects the rim but can invite pull-up jumpers.

Player cue: Big below the ball, guard back in front.

ICE / Down

Side ball-screen coverage that forces the ball away from the middle and down the sideline or baseline, denying use of the screen.

Coach point: The on-ball defender must angle the handler before the screen arrives.

Player cue: No screen, no middle.

Show and recover

A quick hedge where the screener's defender shows a body to the ball and immediately snaps back to the original assignment.

Coach point: Buy the guard one count without giving up the roller.

Player cue: Two steps out, full sprint back.

Under / Over

The on-ball defender's path on a screen: over the top to stay attached to shooters, under the screen against non-shooters.

Coach point: Personnel drives the call.

Player cue: Shooter over, driver under.

Late switch / Peel switch

A delayed exchange after the original coverage breaks down, often used to handle slips, short rolls, or beaten defenders.

Coach point: Emergency switches still need loud ownership.

Player cue: Next takes ball, old defender peels out.

Short roll

The screener rolls into the free-throw line or short-corner area instead of all the way to the rim.

Coach point: Short rolls stress the low man and weak-side rotations.

Player cue: Stop the catch, then fan out.

Off-Ball Screens & Actions

Actions

Down screen / Pin down

A screen set from high to low or from the lane area to free a shooter moving up to the perimeter.

Coach point: Defender decides whether to trail, shoot the gap, switch, or top-lock.

Player cue: Body before the cutter turns the corner.

Flare screen

A screen that sends a teammate away from the ball toward the sideline or corner for a catch-and-shoot.

Coach point: Communication must come from the screener's defender.

Player cue: Hear screen, see shooter.

Back screen

A screen set behind a defender to free a cutter going toward the rim.

Coach point: The defender guarding the screener must call it early.

Player cue: Open up, protect the rim first.

Cross screen

A baseline or block-to-block screen, usually used to free a post player near the rim.

Coach point: Decide whether posts are fighting through, switching, or fronting after contact.

Player cue: Do not get buried under the rim.

Stagger

Two off-ball screens in a row for the same cutter.

Coach point: The second screen is where most defenders relax.

Player cue: Chase through both.

Slip

The screener fakes setting a screen, then cuts hard to space or the rim before establishing contact.

Coach point: Slips punish over-aggressive switches and traps.

Player cue: See the screener's body, not just the ball.

Top-lock

A defender jumps to the high side of a shooter to take away the normal route off a screen and force a backdoor cut.

Coach point: Top-lock requires rim help behind it.

Player cue: Take away the catch, trust the help.

Lock and trail

A defender chases tight behind a shooter coming off a screen, staying attached while help protects the curl.

Coach point: Use against movement shooters you cannot let catch clean.

Player cue: Hip to hip, no gap.

Shoot the gap

A defender cuts between screener and cutter to beat the action to the spot.

Coach point: Works when the screen angle is loose; risky against tight screeners.

Player cue: Beat the route, then recover square.

Switch off-ball

Two defenders exchange assignments away from the ball to stay connected through screening action.

Coach point: The switch is not complete until both matchups are named.

Player cue: Say who you have.

Rotations, Help & Paint Protection

Help-side

Help-side

The side of the floor away from the ball where defenders shrink toward the lane to protect paint touches and cuts.

Coach point: Help-side defenders must still know where shooters are.

Player cue: In early, out fast.

Strong side / Weak side

Strong side is the ball side; weak side is the opposite side where the deepest help usually lives.

Coach point: Use the terms to assign who helps and who recovers first.

Player cue: Ball side pressures, weak side protects.

Tag the roller

A help defender briefly steps into the roller's path to slow the cut, then recovers out to the next threat.

Coach point: Tag with your body, not a lazy reach.

Player cue: Hit the roll, sprint out.

X-out

Two defenders cross and exchange matchups on the fly to cover an open shooter and the next pass after rotation.

Coach point: X-outs save possessions after the ball beats the first help.

Player cue: First takes first, second takes next.

Scram switch

An off-ball switch that pulls a small defender out of a post mismatch after an earlier switch created a bad matchup.

Coach point: Scram before the post entry, not after the catch is deep.

Player cue: Get the guard out.

Shrink / Close the gap

All five defenders pull toward the paint when the ball is above the break, showing bodies to deter drives.

Coach point: Shrink until the ball moves; do not camp in no-man's land.

Player cue: Show a crowd.

Pack the paint

A five-player emphasis on keeping the ball out of the lane and forcing contested shots over the top.

Coach point: Great against drivers, dangerous if shooters get rhythm threes.

Player cue: No layups, contest out.

No-middle

Team rule that forces drives toward the sideline or baseline and refuses straight-line attacks to the middle.

Coach point: Angles matter more than effort; one bad hip opens the middle.

Player cue: Top foot high, send it wide.

Ice the post

Fronting or three-quartering the post while help is loaded from a designated side to discourage deep catches.

Coach point: The passer's pressure and backside help make the post ice work.

Player cue: No deep catch.

Wall up

Verticality at the rim: chest up, arms straight, absorb contact, and contest without swiping down.

Coach point: Make officials see legal vertical position.

Player cue: Tall hands, still chest.

Pressure, Traps & Full-Court Concepts

Pressure

Contain press

Full- or three-quarter-court pressure designed to slow the offense and drain clock without heavy gambling.

Coach point: Winning means pace disruption, not always a steal.

Player cue: Turn them twice.

Run-and-jump

A full-court trap where the on-ball defender corrals the dribble and a second defender jumps to trap.

Coach point: The trap must arrive while the dribble is alive.

Player cue: Corral, jump, rotate.

Side trap / Corner trap

A two-player trap using the sideline or corner as an extra defender.

Coach point: Feet seal escape routes; hands take away vision.

Player cue: Boundary is our teammate.

Denial press

Pressure that takes away the inbound or first pass to force rushed entries, five-second calls, or catches in bad spots.

Coach point: Denial must come with safety help behind it.

Player cue: No easy catch.

Match-up press

A press that starts with man principles and can morph into a zone shape as the ball advances.

Coach point: Players need rules for when to stay, pass off, and recover.

Player cue: Match early, pass off clean.

Safety / Center fielder

The deep defender responsible for long passes behind the press and emergency rim protection.

Coach point: The safety prevents one gamble from becoming a layup.

Player cue: Stay deeper than the deepest threat.

Team Schemes & Coverages

Systems

Man-to-man

Each defender guards a specific offensive player, with team help rules behind the matchup.

Coach point: Man defense still needs help, stunts, tags, and rotations.

Player cue: Guard yours, help ours.

Zone defense

Defenders guard areas and responsibilities instead of one fixed matchup, such as 2-3, 3-2, 1-3-1, box-and-one, or triangle-and-two.

Coach point: Zones break when players guard grass instead of threats.

Player cue: Area first, player next.

Match-up zone

A zone that uses man-to-man principles, matching cutters, handing off assignments, and changing shape as the ball moves.

Coach point: It looks like zone but demands man-level communication.

Player cue: Match, talk, pass off.

Ball-side / Weak-side I

Two help defenders stacked in the lane like an I, protecting the rim and drop-off passes.

Coach point: Useful language for loaded help against spread drives.

Player cue: High help, low help.

Shell drill

Core teaching drill for man defense that emphasizes ball pressure, help, closeouts, and communication.

Coach point: Connect shell rules to live possessions every day.

Player cue: Every pass moves all five.

Kill / Stop

A defensive possession that ends with no score; many programs call three stops in a row a kill.

Coach point: Track stops as team momentum, not just opponent misses.

Player cue: Finish the possession.

No paint touch

Team standard that the ball cannot be dribbled or passed into the lane without a crowd, contest, or trap.

Coach point: Paint touches are advantage creators even when they do not become shots.

Player cue: Protect the lane first.

Communication & Behavior

Talk

Ball, Help, Gap

Simple defensive talk that names who is pressuring the ball, who is protecting the paint, and who is sitting in the driving gap.

Coach point: These three calls organize the floor before the action starts.

Player cue: Say your job out loud.

Shot, Box out

The shot-release call that triggers contact first, then pursuit of the rebound.

Coach point: Rebounding starts on the call, not when the ball hits the rim.

Player cue: Hit, then go get.

Tag, Go, Switch

Short rotation commands used on screens, rolls, drives, and scramble situations.

Coach point: One-syllable calls travel faster during chaos.

Player cue: Command, then move.

Early, Loud, Continuous

The standard for defensive talking: communicate before the action, with enough volume to be heard, and keep talking through the possession.

Coach point: Late talk is usually just a description of a mistake.

Player cue: Talk before they cut.

Next

Call that the next defender in the rotation is taking a cutter, ballhandler, or open threat in a scramble.

Coach point: Next keeps the defense connected when the first plan breaks.

Player cue: I have the next problem.

Rebounding

Possession

Box out

Make contact and seal your player away from the rim before going for the rebound.

Crash

Go aggressively to the offensive glass after a shot.

Release

Guard or wing who leaks out when the shot goes up to ignite the break before the defense can recover.

Hit-and-go-get

Make contact first (hit), then pursue the ball. Emphasis phrase for contested rebounding situations.

Offensive Spacing & Decisions

Offense

5-out / 4-out 1-in

All five players on the perimeter (5-out) vs. four around one post inside (4-out 1-in). The alignment determines spacing and drive lanes.

Drive-and-kick

Attack off the dribble to draw help, then kick out to an open teammate on the perimeter.

Kick-ahead

Pass the ball up the floor in transition instead of dribbling — gets the ball ahead faster than any ball handler can run.

Paint touch

Getting the ball into the lane via drive or pass to collapse the defense and create open kick-outs.

Corner spacing / 45° cut

Corner players hold spacing or cut into the lane at the "45" angle when their defender helps on a drive.

Transition

Tempo

Transition

Phase from defense to offense (or offense to defense) before both teams are fully set.

Primary break

First push up the floor for layups or early threes before the defense can organize and outnumber.

Secondary break

Organized actions — drag screen, trailer, wide pins — that follow the initial break if the primary doesn't produce a shot.

Advantage / Disadvantage

Numbers or closeout situations to recognize and attack: 2-on-1, 3-on-2, or a defender caught on a long closeout.

Baseline Vocabulary

Fundamentals

Man-to-man

Each defender is responsible for guarding a specific offensive player.

Zone

Defenders guard areas of the floor (e.g., 2–3, 1–3–1) rather than individual players.

Turnover

Loss of possession before a shot — via steal, bad pass, violation, or out of bounds.

And-one

Player scores while being fouled and earns one additional free throw attempt.

Field goal % / Free throw %

Share of shots made from the field (FG%) or from the free-throw line (FT%).
